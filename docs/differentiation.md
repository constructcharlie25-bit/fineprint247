# FinePrint vs. "Just paste it into ChatGPT/Claude"

Why a purpose-built contract scanner beats a general chatbot, in exactly eight reasons. This document drives the homepage copy.

## 1. A structured 0–100 risk score, every time

ChatGPT gives you prose — a paragraph, a list, a vibe. FinePrint returns a consistent, comparable **risk score from 0–100** for every contract. Scores are stable across scans, so you can compare this client's MSA against last month's, and a $5 scan of a 40-page SOW produces the same shape of output as a 2-page NDA. Chatbots have no calibrated notion of "risk" unless you invent one in a prompt.

## 2. Severity-tagged clause findings

Every flagged clause carries a severity tag — **high / medium / low** — derived from the clause language itself, not from vibes. ChatGPT will happily list "potential concerns" but buries the deal-breakers next to the nitpicks. FinePrint's report separates the non-compete that could cost you a year of income from the typo in the notices section.

## 3. "Why it matters" + "what to do" for every clause

Flagging a clause is half the job. Each FinePrint finding explains **why the clause matters to you specifically** (e.g., "this indemnity clause makes you liable for the client's legal costs") and gives you a **concrete negotiation suggestion** (e.g., "ask to cap indemnity at the fees paid under this agreement"). ChatGPT often stops at identifying the clause; you have to prompt again — correctly — to get actionable advice.

## 4. Zero prompt engineering required

Getting a good contract review out of ChatGPT takes a carefully engineered prompt: define the role, define the persona ("you are reviewing for a freelance designer"), specify the output format, ask follow-up questions. Most freelancers write "is this contract okay?" and get a shallow thumbs-up. FinePrint's analysis is **contract-tuned end to end** — the system prompt, the extraction, the scoring rubric, and the report format are all purpose-built for contract review. You paste; it scans.

## 5. Pay per scan ($5), not $20/month chatbot subscriptions

ChatGPT Plus is $20/month whether you sign contracts weekly or once a quarter. FinePrint is **$5 per scan** — pay for exactly what you use — or $29/month unlimited for power users. Most freelancers sign a handful of agreements a year; per-scan pricing matches how they actually work.

## 6. No account needed

No signup, no login, no onboarding flow, no "create an account to continue." Paste, scan, read your report. The friction between "client sent me a contract" and "I understand this contract" is one click.

## 7. Contract-tuned system prompt

A general chatbot is a generalist — its contract "review" is an improvisation. FinePrint runs a **contract-specific system prompt and scoring rubric** built around the clauses that actually bite freelancers and small agencies: IP assignment scope, non-competes, indemnity, liability caps, payment terms, termination rights, and kill fees. It's the difference between asking a stranger and asking someone who has read a thousand service agreements.

## 8. Privacy: your contract is never used to train models

Contracts contain rates, client names, and proprietary terms. FinePrint submits text through the API tier where, under OpenAI's API data policy, **submitted data is not used to train models**. ChatGPT's consumer product has historically used conversation data for training unless you opt out — a setting most people never touch. FinePrint defaults to private; there's no toggle to forget.
