Why this clause is risky

Liability is what you owe if something goes wrong. Most fair contracts cap it at what the client paid you. Uncapped means there is no ceiling at all.

One claim, even a weak one, could cost you years of income on a contract worth a few thousand dollars. Also check the direction: many client-drafted contracts cap their liability while leaving yours open. It should be mutual.

The pushback email

Copy this, fill in the brackets, send it.

Subject: Quick ask on the liability section

Hi [Client name],

I am fine with the indemnity in principle, but uncapped means one claim could cost me more than this whole contract pays. I cannot sign that.

Can we cap my total liability at the fees paid under this agreement? Standard stuff, and it keeps us both covered.

Thanks,
[Your name]
